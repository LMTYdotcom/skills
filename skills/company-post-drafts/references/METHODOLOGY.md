# Company Post Drafts Methodology

## What a post from the record can say

A public post is the least forgiving place to be wrong about a date or a speaker: it is read by the company it is about. So a draft says only three kinds of thing.

| Kind of sentence | Needs |
| --- | --- |
| What happened, on a date | An entry whose own date is the event's, and its source |
| What a source reported, on a date | An entry dated by a report, and the source |
| What the company said | The company's own announcement, attributed to it |

Everything else is left to the person posting: the opinion, the comparison with their own company, the call to action.

## The reading budget

At most eight calls.

| Call | What it reads |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | The search for the company |
| 2 | The calendar, with a small `limit`: it gives the `total`, and a short calendar whole |
| 3 | The newest entries of the calendar, as one run of the 30 entries before `total`, only when the first call did not return everything |
| 4 | The timeline of the profile the entries are about, newest first, 50 entries, for their grades and sources |
| 5 | A search for the name the entries' `companies` gives, only when that profile is not one already found. An overview's relations often do not show it |
| 6 to 8 | A second search under the other obvious name; a further timeline page that holds an entry an angle leads with; the calendar of a product under the same name |

A calendar lists the last 90 days earliest first, then what is coming, so its first page is the oldest news it holds. A post is written from the end: the first call exists to learn `total`, and the second reads the newest entries as one unbroken run. When that run is not the whole calendar, the coverage line says "the newest N of M entries, back to [date]". The oldest entries are never given as coverage of the 90 days.

When the budget ends before a list does, stop and report the read as N of M. An angle is built only from entries read.

## Where the grade and the sources are

A calendar entry has neither. They are on the timeline of the profile the entry is about, and its `companies` names that profile. A company's calendar holds its products' entries; a company's timeline holds only the company's own. So for a launch or a price change, the timeline to read is usually the product's.

The two lists do not hold the same entries. A calendar can hold entries its profile's timeline lacks, and a timeline holds entries from before the calendar's 90 days. They can also date one story differently: merge them as "One story, many reports" says.

A timeline full of page readings can put a lead entry far down the list, so ask for 50 entries, and say how many were read of how many.

An entry that is on no timeline page read has no grade to give: write "grade not read". An angle that would lead with it is still offered, marked so and placed after the others, and no draft is written from it unless the user asks. A draft written then says, under its first line, that the grade and sources were not read, and follows each claim from that entry with "[source not read]".

## How a date is known

Each entry carries the answer: `startsAtBasis` on a calendar entry, `atBasis` on a timeline entry. "Dates" in `LMTY.md` defines the values, and says what a page reading is.

| Group | The record says | In an angle or a draft |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dated by when it happened | `occurred` | "on [date]". May lead |
| Dated by a report | `published` | "reported on [date] by [source]". May lead |
| First seen | `observed`, or an entry that reports what a page now lists or now says, whatever its basis | "now lists" or "now reads". Never leads; left out unless asked for by name |
| No longer listed | An entry that reports a page no longer listing a name, whatever its basis | Never. Counted under "Left out and why" |

The basis comes from the field: an entry whose text reads like an announcement can still be first seen. Whether an entry is a page reading comes from what it reports, not from the kind it is filed under: a listing on a directory or a changed tagline, dated by when it happened, is still LMTY reading a page, and never a lead. An upcoming calendar entry has no basis, because its date is the one announced, and it is left to Event Prep.

Many entries first seen on one day means that was the day the profile was first read. None of them happened that day.

**The basis words a date. It does not rank a story.** What is dated by when it happened is mostly a changelog release, an earnings date or a filing. A funding round, an acquisition or a product launch is nearly always dated by a report. A rule that led with the first group would open a post with a monthly update ahead of a funding round. So either group may lead, and the choice among them is made by what the story is and how well it is supported, as "Angles" says.

**A date that may be a month.** An entry dated by when it happened, on the first of a month at midnight, may stand for the whole month: a page titled for a month's updates is dated that way. When the entry's text gives no day, write "in May 2025", not "on May 1".

## Pages that no longer list a name

An entry can say that a leadership page no longer lists a person, a customers page a company, or an integrations page a partner. Its date is the day LMTY first read the page without the name, and eight names can leave one page at one moment. The page can be another company's: "Initech no longer lists Northwind among its customers" sits on Northwind's calendar and says nothing Northwind did.

A public post that leads with one says a chief executive left, or a customer was lost, on a date when a page was edited. So these are in no angle and no draft. They are counted in their own line under "Left out and why".

When the user asks about one by name, it may be said, as what it is: "Northwind's leadership page no longer lists its chief financial officer, as of 2025-05-02 [Northwind's leadership page]". Never "left", "departed", "lost", "ended" or "dropped".

## The words

| Do not write | Write |
| --- | --- |
| "Northwind just launched an audit log." (first seen last week) | Nothing in the draft. Under "Left out": "now lists an audit log (seen on 2025-05-12)" |
| "Northwind raised $40M on May 6." (the article's date) | "Northwind raised $40M, reported on 2025-05-06 by a trade outlet." |
| "Northwind's CFO has left." (a leadership page no longer lists the name) | Nothing in the draft. Under "Left out": "1 page that no longer lists a name" |
| "Northwind lost Initech as a customer." (Initech's page no longer lists Northwind) | Nothing in the draft. It is Initech's page, and a page change |
| "Northwind shipped its May update on May 1." (dated the first of the month, no day in the text) | "Northwind published its May 2025 update [Northwind's changelog, date not given]." |
| "The audit log is now live for all customers." (only the company's post) | "Northwind says its audit log is available to all customers [Northwind's own announcement, 2025-05-02]." |
| "Four announcements this month." (four reports of one launch) | "One launch, reported four times, first on 2025-05-02." |
| "Northwind is doubling down on enterprise." | Nothing. That is a motive |
| "Expect a price rise next." | Nothing. That is a forecast |
| "A huge move for the category." | Nothing, or the company's own word inside a quotation attributed to it |
| "Unlike Northwind, Acme already offers this." | "[YOUR VIEW: how this compares with your own product, yours to write]" |

## One story, many reports

A story is one event. Merge entries that describe the same event, from the calendar and the timeline alike: the same thing launched, the same round, the same sale.

- the story takes the strongest basis among its entries: when it happened, over a report, over first seen
- its date is the earliest date of that basis. One entry dated by when it happened (2025-05-02) and three dated by a report (2025-05-06 to 2025-05-09) are one story, dated 2025-05-02
- four entries that are all dated by a report are one story, "reported on" the earliest of the four dates
- give the number of reports once, in the angle's "Behind it" line and under "Left out"
- two entries that may be one event are counted as one, and the draft never says "twice", "again" or "another"

**Merge first, then cut to the last 90 days.** A story can have an old entry on the timeline and a recent one on the calendar. Cut first and the old entry goes, leaving the story dated by its latest report. Merged first, the story takes its earliest date, and a story first reported before the window is set aside as older, however recent its last report. It is not news.

## Dates and sources

The date of a story is an entry's date, read with its basis. A source's date is never put in its place.

A source has a date of its own, when the record holds one, and it is often not the entry's. An entry keeps its best sources, not the one that dated it, so a source can be published after the entry's date, or well before it. Many company pages carry no publication date at all.

- in the sentence: the story's date, worded by its basis
- in the bracket after the claim: the source, and the source's own publication date, or "date not given"
- a source published before the story's date, plainly about the same event by its title: say so in the angle's "Behind it" line, "the earliest source is dated 2025-03-28, so the story may be older than its entry"
- an older page whose title is about something else: an entry can cite such pages. List it with its own date and say nothing more
- never work out a date from an address, a title or memory

## Whose voice

| Behind the story | The draft says |
| --- | --- |
| Only the company's own announcement, page or account | "Northwind says", "Northwind announced on [date]" |
| Only press | "[outlet] reported on [date] that" |
| The other party to a deal | "[the investor] states", by name |
| The company and press | Both, each with its own source |
| An entry the record grades chatter, whatever its source | Nothing. It is left out and counted |

An official grade shows that one of the parties said it. It does not make the claim the drafter's own. A launch with only the company's own post behind it is the company's statement that it launched.

## Angles

An angle is a sentence the user can choose between, not a headline.

- "Northwind announced an audit log on 2025-05-02, and one outlet reported it the same week."
- "Three price entries on Northwind's record in 90 days: 2025-03-04, 2025-04-15 and 2025-05-02."
- "What Northwind said about its Series B, beside what two outlets reported."

Several entries of one kind are a count with dates. They are not a "trend", a "push" or "momentum".

**Choosing what leads.** Any story dated by when it happened or by a report may lead. Among them:

- what the story is comes first: a funding round, an acquisition, a launch or a disclosed incident before a routine update
- then how well it is supported: the company's own announcement, press, or both. Never an entry graded chatter alone
- then the newest
- say in one clause how the choice was made: "ordered by what the story is, then by support, then newest first"

With one dated story on record there is one angle. Say so: "The record supports one angle."

## A worked draft

For a social post, first angle above:

> Draft, for a person to review before it is posted. Nothing has been posted or sent.
>
> Northwind announced an audit log for its Team plan on May 2 [Northwind's own announcement, 2025-05-02]. Northwind says it records every permission change and keeps 12 months of history [Northwind's own announcement, 2025-05-02]. One trade outlet reported the release on May 6 [a trade outlet, date not given].
>
> [YOUR VIEW: one sentence on what this means for your readers, yours to write]

The same request worded as "write a hot take" gets the same draft and one more line: "The opinion is yours to add where the gap is marked: the record supports what happened, not what to think of it."

## The short form

When no story is dated by when it happened or by a report, once the pages that no longer list a name are set aside:

> Read all 14 calendar entries for Northwind on 2025-05-20. 13 are first seen and 1 is a page that no longer lists a name: none is dated by when it happened or by a report.
>
> Nothing on record carries a date of its own.
>
> - now lists an audit log (seen on 2025-05-12)
> - now lists a Team plan (seen on 2025-05-12)
> - 1 page no longer lists a name, as of 2025-05-14

No angles and no draft follow. Do not offer a draft "anyway". A calendar that holds nothing but another company's page change gets the same form.

When nothing on the profile falls in the last 90 days at all, the coverage line and one sentence saying so are the whole answer.

## Length

Two or three angles of one sentence each, one draft, and at most six lines of what was left out. A second or third draft is written only when asked for.
