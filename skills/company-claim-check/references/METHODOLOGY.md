# Claim Check Methodology

## What a check can establish

A check answers three questions about a claim: who says it, how many independent voices say it, and how recently. From those it gives a verdict about the claim's support. It does not establish whether the claim is true, and its wording must never suggest that it has.

## The verdict scale

The eight verdicts and their tests are in `TRUST.md`, under "Verdicts on a claim", where every skill that judges a claim reads them. Use the label exactly, then one sentence of reasons that names the sources and their dates:

- "Supported: the certifying body's register lists it, checked 2024-11-19, and the company's security page says the same."
- "The subject's own claim: Northwind's homepage says 'more than 10,000 customers' (read 2024-11-19). No independent source gives a count."
- "Reported, not confirmed by the subject: two outlets give the round as $50M (2024-03). Initech's own pages do not state an amount."
- "Unverified: one forum post says so (2024-10-02)."
- "Disputed: one outlet gives Northwind's Series B as $40M, another gives the same round as $55M (both 2024-03)."
- "Out of date: Globex was listed as a customer until 2024-06; no longer listed as of 2024-09-12."
- "Not observed in LMTY's sources as of 2024-11-19. That is not evidence against it."
- "Untestable: 'the leading platform' names no measure. A testable form would be a ranked list and its date."

Supported needs more than press. An independent source supports a claim when it is in a position to know: a register, a filing, the other party's own statement. Press alone, however many outlets, is "Reported, not confirmed by the subject".

For a claim that one company uses another's product, the interested party is the vendor, and the second verdict is worded for it: "The vendor's own claim: Northwind's customers page lists Globex (read 2024-11-19). Nothing from Globex's side shows it."

## Pinning a claim down

A testable claim has a subject, a measure, a value and a date.

| As made | Restated |
| --- | --- |
| "They have 10,000 customers." | "Northwind had at least 10,000 paying customers as of the date of the deck (2024-09)." |
| "They're SOC 2 certified." | "Northwind holds a current SOC 2 report; type and period unknown." |
| "Globex uses them." | "Globex is a current customer of Northwind's product." |
| "They raised a Series B." | "Initech closed a round named Series B; amount and date unstated." |

Restating often shows the claim was vaguer than it sounded. Put what you had to assume in the restatement, and say you assumed it.

## Units that hide inside numbers

Most disputes about a number are disputes about what it counts.

- customers, paying customers, users, teams, accounts, sign-ups, downloads
- employees, full-time employees, people on a networking site who list the company
- raised in a round, raised in total, valuation
- annual recurring revenue, revenue, bookings, run rate
- a certification held, a report issued, an audit in progress

When the claim uses one unit and the evidence another, the verdict is on the evidence's unit, and the gap section says so.

## Two numbers that are not in conflict

A company's claim of "10,000 customers" and a profile holding 412 known by name do not dispute each other. One is a total the company states; the other is how many have been seen in public. Report both, say what each is, and give the verdict on the claim: usually "the subject's own claim".

"Disputed" is for two figures that count the same thing.

## Reading the standing of a source

| Tier | What it is good for |
| --- | --- |
| Official | What the subject says. Strong for its prices, plans, people and policies. Weak, alone, for anything that flatters it |
| Press | That an event happened and roughly when. Figures in press are often the company's own, repeated |
| Credible | A known analyst or writer. One voice |
| Unverified | That somebody said it |

Two articles that trace to one press release are one voice. Say so when you can see it.

LMTY's own confidence reflects how often and how recently its sources said the thing. Cite it as LMTY's. A claim marked uncertain while still graded official usually means a source has stopped listing it: treat that as a prompt to check the dates.

## Tense

A present-tense claim needs a recent source. Decide what recent means from how fast the thing changes: weeks for prices and open roles, months for headcount and leadership, a year or more for founding facts.

When the latest source is older than that, the verdict is "Out of date" if something has since changed. If nothing has been seen since, keep the verdict the evidence earns and put the date in the sentence of reasons: "as of 2023-11, the latest source; nothing observed since".

## What would settle it

Name the specific thing, not a category:

| Claim | Would settle it |
| --- | --- |
| A certification | The certifying body's register, or the report's cover page under NDA |
| A named customer | The customer's own statement, case study or job ad |
| A funding amount | A filing, or both the company and the lead investor stating the same figure |
| A headcount | A filing or a dated statement by the company |
| A customer count | A filing, or an audited figure. Often nothing public will |

When nothing public would settle it, say so. That is useful to know before repeating the claim.

## The correction gate

A correction is drafted only when the user asks for one, and only when the record holds a source that is official, dated, and about the same thing as the claim while saying something different. "The same thing" is the exact claim (the same figure, plan name or statement), the same plan or product where the claim names one, and the same scope: region, billing period, currency, date or period.

For a post that says "Northwind's Pro plan is $50 a seat a month":

| The record holds | Gate |
| --- | --- |
| Northwind's pricing page, official, last seen 2024-11-19: Pro at "$30 per seat per month" | Passes |
| A trade article giving $30 | Fails: reported, not official |
| An official page with the figure and no date on record | Fails: no date |
| The official price of the Team plan, or of Pro billed annually, or in another currency or region | Fails: a different plan or scope |
| Nothing on Pro's price | Fails: no official source |

A draft that passes reads: "Draft, for review before posting: Northwind's pricing page lists Pro at '$30 per seat per month' (last seen 2024-11-19). The post gives $50." It quotes the source for the figure, gives the source and its date, and stops. From anyone but Northwind it stays "Northwind's pricing page lists", never "Pro costs".

When the gate fails the answer is "No correction drafted", the part that failed, and the one thing that would make a correction possible. No suggested response takes its place. A verdict of Untestable, and a claim the company itself made, never get a correction: there is nothing checkable to set against the first, and the company's official source is the second.

## Tone

Write as though the person who made the claim will read it. State what they said, what stands behind it and what does not. No adjectives about them.
