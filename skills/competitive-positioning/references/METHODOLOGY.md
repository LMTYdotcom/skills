# Competitive Positioning Methodology

## The central rule

Positioning is a context decision before it is a writing exercise.

The goal is to make the product easy to understand and easy to compare against the right alternatives.

## Positioning sequence

1. Competitive alternatives
2. Unique attributes
3. Customer value created by those attributes
4. Customer segment that cares most
5. Market/category frame
6. Proof and reasons to believe
7. Messaging implications

Changing the order tends to produce generic copy because the team writes claims before understanding the context that makes the claims meaningful.

## Customer progress

Use Jobs to Be Done as a causal lens, not a fill-in-the-blank template.

Look for:
- the moment the old way became insufficient
- what the customer was trying to accomplish
- anxieties about switching
- what success looks like in the customer's language

## Market creation versus capture

### Market capture

Use when buyers already understand the category and actively compare solutions.

Positioning should make the comparison dimensions and advantage obvious.

### Market creation

Use when the buyer does not yet organize the problem around the category.

Positioning needs to teach the problem or new possibility before asking the buyer to compare products.

Many products combine both. A segment may be market capture while a new use case requires market creation. State the dominant motion rather than forcing purity.

## Differentiation quality test

A useful differentiator passes four tests:

1. True
2. Relevant
3. Provable
4. Meaningfully different in the chosen comparison context

A durable differentiator adds a fifth:

1. Hard enough to erase, copy, or neutralize that the company can build around it

Not every positioning advantage needs to be a moat. Do not confuse a compelling reason to buy now with a durable business moat.

## Positioning stress tests

### Wrong-alternative test

If customers are comparing the product to something else, the position is anchored in the wrong context.

### Feature-copy test

If a competitor copied the headline feature tomorrow, would the position collapse?

### Customer-language test

Would a buyer recognize the struggle and value without learning internal product vocabulary?

### Sales-explanation test

Can a salesperson explain the position in under a minute without inventing unsupported claims?

### Market-change test

Would the position survive a competitor pricing change, feature launch, or messaging shift?

If not, identify which part is fragile.
