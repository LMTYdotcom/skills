# Company Briefing Methodology

## What a briefing is for

A briefing replaces twenty minutes of searching before a meeting. Its reader will act on it within the hour and will not check it. That sets the two rules everything else follows from: it must be short, and nothing in it may be firmer than its source.

## Reading budget

The overview, plus at most four more reads. A briefing that needed ten sections is a deep dive. The budget counts sections, not calls: a further page of a section already being read is not a new read, and a section that is read is read to its end or reported with both numbers.

Start from the overview's section counts. Then choose by purpose:

| The briefing is for | Read after the overview | Pull forward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No purpose given | changes, pricing, connections, calendar | The basics, leadership, what moved |
| A sales call with this company as the prospect | connections, people, hiring, changes | Who leads it, what tools it is known to use, what it is hiring for |
| A partnership or integration talk | connections, statements, changes, calendar | Its partners and integrations, what its leaders say it is building |
| An investor or board conversation | facts (funding and financial kinds), connections, trends, occasions | Rounds, backers, headcount over time, what it told the market |
| Meeting a person | the person's own profile: overview, statements, portfolio, people | Current roles, career, what they have said recently |
| Background on a competitor | changes, pricing, customers (on the product), calendar | What it sells, who uses it, what moved |
| A journalist's or analyst's backgrounder | timeline, statements, facts | Dated events and who said what |

When the subject is a company and the question touches what it sells, the product's profile holds the customers and often the pricing. That is one of the four reads, not an extra.

## Writing a graded sentence

The grade is part of the sentence. Three patterns cover almost everything:

- official: "Northwind lists three plans, the cheapest at $12 a seat a month (its pricing page, read 2024-11-19)."
- reported: "It raised a Series B in March 2024, reported by two trade outlets."
- unverified: "Unverified: one forum post says the Enterprise plan starts near $40 a seat."

Things to avoid:

| Do not write | Because | Write |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Northwind has 10,000 customers." | That is its own marketing number | "Northwind says it has 10,000 customers (homepage, read 2024-11-19). LMTY knows 46 by name." |
| "Northwind dropped its free plan." | The source is a page that stopped listing it | "The free plan is no longer listed on its pricing page as of 2024-11-02." |
| "Northwind recently partnered with Initech." | The entry was first seen last week; the partnership may be years old | "Initech is listed as a partner (first seen 2024-11-12; start date not stated)." |
| "Its CTO left." | Inferred from a later role elsewhere | "Its former CTO now lists a role at Globex; no source states a departure date." |
| "Customers love it." | One post | Leave it out, or quote the post as one post |

## Choosing what moved

A profile's change list mixes two things: real changes, and things read for the first time. Only the first is news.

Keep an item when:

- it was added to, removed from or returned to a page that had been read before
- it has a date a source states, and the date is recent
- a second source or the calendar corroborates it

Then rank by what a reader walking into a meeting would be embarrassed not to know: a new chief executive, a price change, an acquisition, a funding round, a big customer won or lost, a launch announced. Routine entries - one more integration on a long list, one more logo - are counted, not listed: "eleven integrations added since September".

Say the selection is yours: "Selected from 40 changes in the last 180 days, by how much each would change a first conversation."

## The what-changed form

For a request that asks only what is new with one subject. It answers one question: what does LMTY's record date inside the period? It is filled in from the what-changed template, not cut down from a briefing.

Reading:

- the calendar holds the last 90 days, earliest first, with a company's products' entries beside its own: read it from its newest end. The timeline is newest first and goes back further: read it until its entries are older than the period, then stop. `references/LMTY.md` says how to page both
- a period longer than 90 days is read from the timeline alone, and the period line says so
- a calendar entry's grade and sources are on the timeline of the profile it is about. An entry on no timeline page read is "grade not read"
- the first line counts each list from the calls made: "Calendar: read 41 of 41 entries. Timeline: read 50 of 212, back to 2025-06-02."

Sort every entry by its basis field, never by its wording:

| The entry | Where it goes |
| --- | --- |
| Basis `occurred`, and not a page reading | "What happened", under the event's own date |
| Basis `published` | "What happened", as "reported on [date]" |
| Basis `observed` | "First seen by LMTY in the period", counted |
| A page reading, whatever its basis: what a page now lists, now says or no longer lists | "First seen by LMTY in the period", counted |
| Announced as coming | Left out, and counted in the last line |
| No basis field in the answer | As "Dates" in `references/LMTY.md` says for a server without the field |

Then:

- several entries about one event are one row. It takes the strongest basis among them (when it happened, over a report, over first seen) and the earliest date of that basis
- with more than eight rows, choose as "Choosing what moved" says and name the count: "the 8 chosen of 14, by how much each would change a first conversation". The rest are one counted line
- the first-seen lines give counts by kind and the dates seen: "LMTY first read 23 entries between 2025-09-02 and 2025-09-04: now lists 14 integrations, 6 customers and 3 certifications". First-seen dates cluster on the day a page was first read. Give the count and the dates, and say nothing about what a cluster suggests
- the last line names what was not read: the sections outside the two lists, the timeline entries older than the last one read, and the number of entries announced as coming

## Numbers

- give a number with its date and its source, every time
- two sources, two numbers: show both
- a trend needs two dated points from the same source: "open roles went from 31 to 58 between 2024-08-19 and 2024-11-18, on its own job board"
- a count of what LMTY knows (customers, partners, people) is a floor, and is written as one

## People

A briefing names people in their roles: who founded, who leads, who joined from where, who invested. It quotes what they said in public, with the date and the venue.

It does not include anything about a person's private life, and it does not go to other sources to find it. If the user asks for that, say that the briefing covers the public professional record and stop there.

## Length

- the whole briefing: one page, at most 700 words, not counting the Sources list. Count before sending. Over that, cut in the order the skill gives under "Required outcome", and say in the freshness line what was cut
- opening paragraph: three or four sentences
- basics: a table of five to eight rows
- people: three to six names
- what moved: three to six items
- what is coming: every announced item inside the next quarter; later ones in one line
- not observed: as long as it needs to be. This is the one section that should not be cut for length
- the what-changed form: one screen. At most eight rows, one or two first-seen lines, one line for what was not read
