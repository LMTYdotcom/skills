# Launch Competitive Readiness Methodology

## Why this job exists

Product launches are one of the highest-consequence moments for competitive awareness. A launch can be technically strong and still fail to create the intended market perception if the team misunderstands what customers already expect, what alternatives already offer, or what changed during the launch cycle.

The job is not to make every launch sound differentiated. The job is to make the launch **competitively legible**.

## Core questions

### Customer progress

- What job is the customer hiring this capability to do?
- What struggle or critical event makes the capability matter now?
- Which alternatives are already hired for the same progress?
- What would make a buyer switch or stay with the present solution?

### Market expectation

Distinguish:
- capability existence
- quality of execution
- packaging and availability
- proof of value
- customer expectation

A competitor having a feature does not automatically make it table stakes. Repeated customer expectations, repeated deal objections, and converging market behavior are stronger signals.

### Differentiation

Use six lenses when relevant:
- feature or capability
- pricing or packaging
- target segment
- technology that creates a user-visible advantage
- channel or distribution
- customer experience

Then ask whether the difference is defensible or merely temporary.

### Switching dynamics

For the launch's target customer, map:
- push of the current situation
- pull of the new capability
- anxiety about adoption
- habit or switching friction

Launch messaging should strengthen pull and reduce anxiety without manufacturing pain that evidence does not support.

## Launch readiness scorecard

Rate each 0-2, then explain rather than hiding behind the number.

1. Market understanding
2. Customer problem evidence
3. Competitive currency
4. Differentiation clarity
5. Claim support
6. Objection readiness
7. Pricing/packaging coherence
8. Cross-functional readiness

The score is a diagnostic aid, not the output itself.

## Launch posture patterns

### Market-catching launch

Characteristics:
- closes an obvious gap
- competitors have mature alternatives
- customers already expect the capability

Recommended posture:
- avoid breakthrough language
- lead with how the capability fits the broader product advantage
- prepare for "finally" reactions

### Market-leading launch

Characteristics:
- clear differentiated outcome
- proof exists or can be demonstrated
- few credible equivalents

Recommended posture:
- teach the new possibility
- define the comparison dimensions
- collect proof quickly because the advantage may compress

### Narrative-catching launch

Characteristics:
- product capability existed but the market credits a competitor with the story

Recommended posture:
- focus on proof and integrated value
- avoid defensive "we had it first" messaging unless chronology itself matters

### Expectation-creating launch

Characteristics:
- adjacent players or new technology create a new buyer expectation
- direct competitors may not yet offer the capability

Recommended posture:
- explain the job and why now
- avoid overclaiming category inevitability

## Output variants

### Launch brief insert

Compact:
- competitive context
- top 3 implications
- claims and guardrails
- objections
- evidence

### Full readiness review

Use the default asset template.

### Leadership review

Compress to:
- what changed
- where we stand
- key risk
- decision or investment question
