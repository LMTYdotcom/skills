# Competitive Battlecard Methodology

## Battlecard design principle

Battlecards are often requested and often ignored. The skill should optimize for the moment of use, not for completeness.

A useful card combines:
- current market truth
- customer/deal truth
- short language a rep can say
- discovery that exposes fit
- honest qualification guidance

## Core card anatomy

1. Thirty-second competitor orientation
2. When we see them
3. Why customers choose them
4. Three primary differentiation themes
5. Discovery / trap-setting questions
6. Top objections and response posture
7. Proof and customer examples
8. Where they are stronger / when we may lose
9. Recent changes
10. Source freshness

## "Quick dismiss" caution

Traditional battlecards often include a dismissive one-liner. Avoid cheap shots. Prefer a fair framing that redirects the buyer toward decision criteria where the home product has a real advantage.

## Customer evidence hierarchy

Strongest for field guidance:
1. repeated win/loss pattern
2. repeated call or CRM pattern
3. validated switch story
4. individual deal example
5. PMM hypothesis based on public differences

The last category can be useful, but label it as such.

## Conversation design

A rep usually needs:
- one framing sentence
- one or two discovery questions
- one proof point

The deep detail belongs behind the live layer.

## When to walk away

A high-trust battlecard tells sales when not to force a fit.

Examples:
- buyer requires a capability the home product does not have
- segment economics make the competitor structurally better
- switching cost overwhelms the available value

This is a feature, not a weakness, of the battlecard.
