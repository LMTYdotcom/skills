# Customer Map Methodology

## Three numbers that are not the same

| Number | What it is | How to write it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claimed | What the company says about itself | "Northwind says 'more than 10,000 teams' (homepage, read 2024-11-19)" |
| Publicly named | Customers a profile holds by name, each on someone's claim | "412 customers are publicly named as of 2024-11-19" |
| Read | How many of those you actually read | "This map covers the first 100" |

A map that blurs them reads as though 100 companies were the customer base.

A claimed figure can be stored as a bare value with a short label and no sentence. Write it as stored: "Northwind's homepage carries the figure '60%' with the label 'large retailers' (read 2024-11-19); the record does not hold the sentence it came from". Do not add the verb the sentence would have had.

## Kinds of evidence, strongest first for "uses it today"

| Evidence | Who makes the claim | What it supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The product found on the customer's own site or in its code | Nobody: it was observed | In use when read. The strongest evidence of current use |
| The customer's job ad naming the product | The customer | In use or being adopted, around the ad's date |
| A case study or testimonial with the customer quoted | The vendor, with the customer's consent | A real relationship at the time it was written. Says little about today |
| A logo on the vendor's customers page | The vendor | The vendor claims them. Logos stay up long after contracts end |
| A press or third-party article | A third party | Reported use, as of the article |
| A single post | One person | Unverified |

Say the kind, and who makes the claim, for every customer you name. A customer list is the vendor's own claim unless the customer or an independent source says so. When the vendor's page is the only evidence, write "Northwind names Globex as a customer on its customers page", not "Globex uses Northwind" and not "Northwind's customers include Globex".

The heading and the column say "publicly named customers". The claimant is the vendor, the customer itself, or a third party, and it is written into the row.

## Dates

- "since 2022" only when a source states the start
- "seen since 2024-10-13" when the date is when the use was first recorded
- an ended use: "ended, per [how it is known], [date]"

When most start dates fall on one day, that day is when the product's pages were first read. Say so once and do not chart it.

## Segments

Use the sector, size band and country the profile gives for each customer. Report three things per cut: the counts, the number unknown, and the base.

> By sector, of the 100 read: software 38, financial services 17, commerce 9, other 12, sector not given 24.

Rules:

- no percentages when the base is under 50. Counts are clearer and harder to misquote
- "unknown" is a row, never dropped. A cut where a third is unknown is a weak cut, and the reader should see that
- do not rank sectors by fit or value. The map shows where customers are known, not where the product sells best

## Why the known set is skewed

Say this in the limits section, in the words that apply:

- companies that permit logo use are over-represented; regulated and very large buyers often do not
- the vendor chooses which case studies to publish
- detection on sites and in code only finds products that leave a public trace
- job ads only exist for companies that are hiring

## Movement

A customer "added" this month on a page read for months is news. A customer "first seen" is not: it may have been a customer for years.

For a switch, three things must be in the record: an end with one product, a start with the other, and the order. Two of three is "also recorded with".

When a monthly series of customers found and lost is present, give the numbers and what is counted, and note that a spike in "found" in the first month of reading is the backlog, not growth.

## Overlap

Overlap counts companies publicly named for both products, among the markets LMTY reads. The overlap entry does not say who makes the second claim. Report it as a count over the publicly named base. Do not turn it into "X% of their customers also use Y", and do not read direction into it without the dates.

## Answering "is this company a customer?"

- found, with evidence: name the evidence, its date and who makes the claim
- found, ended: say when and how the end is known
- not found: "not observed as a customer in LMTY's sources as of [date]". Offer what would settle it: the vendor's customer page, the company's job ads, a direct question
