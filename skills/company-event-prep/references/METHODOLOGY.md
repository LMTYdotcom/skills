# Event Prep Methodology

## What can be known before an event

| Known | Not known |
| --- | --- |
| That it is happening, and when | What will be announced |
| Its format: a call, a keynote, a booth, an effective date | Whether a rumored launch is on the agenda |
| What the company did at earlier ones | Whether it will do the same again |
| What it has said publicly it will cover | Anything it has not said |

Prep is built entirely from the left column. The right column is where prep sheets usually go wrong.

## Kinds of date

| Kind | What it reliably carries | Prepare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Earnings call | Numbers, guidance, a prepared narrative, questions from analysts | Last quarter's figures and themes to compare against; a list of what would be new |
| Own conference or keynote | Product announcements, roadmap, customer stories | Current versions of every asset that compares products; a watcher per track |
| Appearance at someone else's event | A talk, a booth, sometimes a launch timed to it | The session title and speaker; low priority unless a launch is stated |
| Announced launch | The thing announced, on or near the date | What is stated about it so far; the assets it could make stale |
| Price change taking effect | The new price, for the customers it applies to | The old and new figures; which talk tracks quote the old one |
| Regulatory or contractual date | A deadline that forces a change | What the company has said it will do |

Something the company says is coming with no date ("coming soon") is none of these: there is no date to prepare for. List it under "Announced without a date", with who said it and when, and write no per-date block for it.

## Stated and expected

Write two lists per date and keep them apart on the page.

- **Stated** has a source for every line.
- **Expected** has a count of past occasions behind every line, with their dates: "its last three calls gave headcount". It never says what will be said.

If an "expected" line has no count you can write down, delete it. Fewer than three distinct occasions is too few to say: give what was said as precedent, quoted.

## Precedent

For each coming date, find the last two or three of the same kind and report:

- the date
- the tone, where the profile records one, with a quote behind it
- the two or three heaviest themes
- anything concrete that was announced, with its source
- what followed within two weeks, if the record shows it: a price change, a launch, a hire

Then count. "Twice in the last three" is a precedent. "Once" is an anecdote and is written as one.

An occasion is an earnings call, a keynote, a conference or a launch event. A release note or a changelog entry is not one: it is not precedent for a call or a keynote, and it does not count toward a rhythm.

How an occasion landed is not part of what the company said. It comes only from recorded public reaction: posts on the profile dated after the occasion that name it, or the sibling skill that reads a signed-in user's collected posts. With none: "no public reaction recorded".

Never write "so they will". Write "the last three did".

## Building the prep

**Before.** Ask what a statement on the day could make wrong. Name those things: the comparison page, the battlecard line on price, the talk track that says they lack a feature. Having the current versions in hand is the whole of "before".

**On the day.** One named watcher, the place to watch, and two or three things to listen for. More than three and nothing gets heard.

**After.** Within a day: what was said, quoted; what changed on their site; which assets are now stale. Then the hand-off.

Write each prep line as an option or a check the reader may choose ("Option: ...", "Worth checking: ..."), not as an instruction. The table names what each line is about, not its wording.

By role:

| Reader | Before | On the day | After |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sales | Current card and price lines for this rival | Prices, named customers, claims a buyer will repeat | Refresh talk tracks; brief the team |
| Product | Current view of their roadmap as stated | Dates, scope, what is generally available and what is preview | Compare against own roadmap |
| Marketing | Current comparison and messaging pages | New category words, new proof numbers | Check own pages for stale claims |
| Executive | Last occasion's numbers and narrative | Guidance, strategy statements, anything about the market | A short brief on what changed |

## Tripwires

A tripwire is a kind of statement, named in advance, with the piece of work it could trigger, offered as an option.

Good tripwires are specific and checkable:

- "A list price for the new tier" - option: reprice the comparison and refresh the card
- "General availability, with a date" - option: move from "announced" to "shipping" in every asset
- "A named customer in financial services" - option: tell the two reps with deals there
- "Guidance cut" - option: brief leadership

Poor tripwires are moods: "if they sound aggressive", "if it seems big".

## Dates move

Give each date with when it was last confirmed and by whom. For anything more than a month out, add "check again nearer the time". When a date in the record has passed with nothing recorded against it, say "date passed; outcome not observed", not "did not happen".

## Length

One block per date. A company with nine coming dates gets nine short blocks, the nearest three in full and the rest as a table.
