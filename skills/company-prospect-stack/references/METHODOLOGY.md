# Prospect Stack Methodology

## What public evidence of a stack is

Nobody publishes their vendor list. What exists is a set of traces: a script on a website, a tool named in a job ad, a logo a vendor was allowed to use, a quote in a case study, a line in an article. Each trace has someone making the claim, a date and a half-life.

A stack list is those traces, sorted by how far a seller can lean on each.

## Who makes the claim

| Evidence | Who makes the claim | Strength for "uses it now" | Half-life |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Found on the account's own site or in its code | Nobody: observed | Strong, as of the read date | Short: it is re-read, so a recent date means recent use |
| The account's job ad names the tool | The account | Strong around the ad's date; also shows which team | Months |
| A case study or testimonial quoting a named person at the account | Vendor and account | Strong when written | A year or two |
| A case study or testimonial only the vendor published, with no named person at the account quoted | The vendor | Moderate | Long, and unknowable |
| A logo or name on the vendor's customers page | The vendor | Moderate. Logos stay up after contracts end | Long, and unknowable |
| An article or third-party list | A third party | Weak to moderate | As of the article |
| One post | One person | Weak | - |

Write who makes the claim into the line: "found on Globex's own site", "named in Globex's own job ad for a data engineer", "listed by Northwind on its customers page". A vendor's list is the vendor's own claim until the account or an independent source says so. "Both" needs the account itself saying it uses the tool: its own page, its own code, or a quote from a named person at the account.

The list is headed "publicly evidenced tools", not "its stack" or "the tools it uses".

## Mention, ask, ignore

| Bin | Rule | How a seller uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mention | The account's side shows it, or both sides say it, and it was seen in the last few months | "I saw you're hiring for people with Northwind experience" |
| Ask | The vendor claims it, an article reports it, or it has not been seen lately. Also an item graded chatter, or one whose evidence was not read: neither is ever written as "also uses" or "is known to use" | "Are you still on Northwind for this?" |
| Ignore | Ended long ago | Not raised |

When in doubt, ask. A question costs nothing; a wrong assertion costs the meeting.

## Dates

- "since 2022" only for a start a source states
- "seen since 2024-10-13" for a first-recorded date
- always give "last seen"
- many items first seen on the same day means that was the day the account's profile was first read

An item whose last sighting is older than about a year moves from mention to ask, whatever its evidence.

## Grouping

Group by what each product does only when the product's profile says what it does. Use its label as given. Do not assign categories from memory, and do not decide that two listed products compete.

When labels are missing, list the products alphabetically. An honest flat list is better than a tidy taxonomy that is partly invented.

## What is never visible

Say this in the limits section, in the words that fit the account:

- back-office systems: finance, payroll, HR, legal
- security tooling, by design
- anything bought by one team and used behind a login
- the size, term and renewal date of any contract
- who chose it and who owns it

So a stack list is densest where the account faces the public and thinnest where budgets are often largest.

## The home product

When the user has said what they sell:

- **already there:** say so first, with the evidence. The call is an expansion or renewal conversation, not a prospecting one
- **a named competitor is there:** only when the user named it as a competitor. Then give its evidence and dates in full; this is the incumbent
- **an integration partner is there:** only when a profile shows the integration. That is an opening line
- **a category is named, and no product:** build no section of likely incumbents from integration ties. An integration tie says two products connect, not that the account uses either. Say that integrations are not evidence of use, give any integration with a product its profile places in that category in one line as "connects with, use not evidenced", and make it a question for the call

Do not work out the competitive set yourself. The user's tracked competitors are in their own account, and the skill that reads those is a different one.

## Questions for the call

Each question should be answerable in a sentence and tied to one line of the list.

| From | Question |
| --- | --- |
| An "ask" item | "Are you still using [product] for [what its label says]?" |
| A "mention" item with no owner known | "Who looks after [product] on your side?" |
| A long-running item | "When does that come up for renewal?" |
| Two listed products that seem to do the same job | "How do you split work between [A] and [B]?" |
| A gap in a visible category | "What are you using for [category] today?" |

Three to five. A seller reads these on the way in.

## People

A stack list names companies and products. It names a person only by a public role held on a profile, and only when the user asks who to speak to. It does not look up individuals, contact details or anything about them beyond that role.

## Length

The table, the questions, and four lines of limits. If the account has sixty tools on record, show the fifteen most relevant to what the user sells, count the rest, and offer the full list.
