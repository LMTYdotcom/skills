# Quote Finder Methodology

## What a candidate is

A candidate is one public post, returned by LMTY in this run, whose excerpt praises the home product, written by someone with no visible tie to the company. Signed in, it is also a post that matched a phrase LMTY monitors for the product itself.

It is a lead. Between a candidate and a published quote stand three things this skill cannot do: learn who the person is, learn whether they use the product, and get their permission. Each candidate says so.

## The product's own phrases

An account collects what its phrases match. A phrase is the product's own only when its kind says so. An account can have dozens of phrases and none of its own: every one a competitor's name or domain, or a field term. Its positive posts are then praise of competitors, and a read of them finds no lead.

| The phrase list holds | The read |
| --- | --- |
| No phrase of the product's own | "LMTY monitors no phrase for Acme itself, so praise of it is not collected through this account. The 30 phrases it monitors are competitors' and the field's." When the removed list holds phrases of its own, add: "Its own phrases, 'Acme' and 'acme.example', were removed on 2025-02-13." Then the free form for the product, when it has a public profile. Its coverage line drops the sentence about what an account would add: the reader has one, and what it lacks is a phrase |
| Own phrases, all paused | Say so. Read as usual: the period can hold posts from before the pause, which has no date |
| Own phrases, some removed in the last 90 days | Give each with the date it was removed. Its posts are kept and counted |
| Own phrases, active | Steps 3 and 4 |

With own phrases, the counts and the pages are both narrowed to them, as the posts reference says under "The product's own posts". Three things follow for this read:

- **M is the positive count of the narrowed counts.** The pages are narrowed the same way and to the same sentiment, so N and M count the same posts
- **One pass, not one for each tag.** A pass for user feedback and another for mentions of the home brand was a way to find the product's posts among everyone's. Narrowed, every page is the product's. Two passes would read a post with both tags twice, leave out a positive post with neither, and need a count of their own for each tag
- **Tags still set posts aside.** The promotional, machine-written, first-party and directory tags come on each post

On a server that does not narrow, the pages are positive posts about everything the account monitors. Filter them as the posts reference says under "Check the echo". The coverage line then reads "read 140 positive posts, back to 2025-03-09, to find 23 that matched one of Acme's own phrases; total not known", and the row "Matched no phrase of the product's own" appears under "Set aside".

## The 12 calls

**Signed in:**

| Order | Calls | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | Which product the connection reads |
| 2 | 1 | The phrase list: which phrases are the product's own |
| 3 | 1 | The counts for the period, narrowed to the product's own phrases |
| 4 | 1 | The search for the home product's public profile |
| 5 | 1 | Its overview, for the official accounts |
| 6 | Up to 7 | Positive posts that matched an own phrase, a page at a time, until they end |

Ask for the largest page. The posts reference says how many posts such a page holds, which is fewer than asked for: seven pages are about 210 to 280 posts, every one of them positive and the product's own. For most products that is all of them in the period, and the read ends with calls unspent. Count what came back, and go by whether the answer says more are left. When more are left, say how far back the pages reached and that the days before were not read.

When no profile carries exactly the product's name, the search is the only call spent on it. Only the product's overview is read in this form: an account listed on the company's profile alone is not known, and the answer says the company's profile was not read.

When Step 2 finds no phrase of the product's own, two calls are spent and ten are left: enough for the free form, with two pages of the company's posts at most.

**Free:**

| Order | Calls | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | The search for the product |
| 2 | 1 | The product's overview: the official accounts, and the company that makes it |
| 3 | 1 | The company's overview: its accounts, and whether it shows any posts |
| 4 | Up to 5 | The product's public posts, a page at a time, until all that are shown are read |
| 5 | Up to 4 | The company's public posts, only when its overview shows some |

A company's profile rarely shows posts: they sit on the product. So no call is held for them. The free form reaches every post shown for the product, which is at most 100.

A page can hold fewer posts than asked for. Go by what the answer says is left, and count the posts received.

## The coverage line

Signed in:

> Read 90 of 90 positive posts that matched one of Acme's own phrases ("Acme", "acme.example") in the 30 days to 2025-03-14, from 74 authors; 2 had no handle. Those phrases matched 310 posts in the period, from 240 authors. Social listening began on 2024-11-02.

Free:

> Read from Acme's public posts on LMTY: 100 of the 4,300 collected in the last 30 days. The 4,300 are from 2,900 authors; the 100 read are from 81, and 6 had no handle. These are the most engaged posts, not a sample, and carry no sentiment: praise was judged by reading. An LMTY account with Social Listening on would add every collected post, with sentiment, tags and any period within 90 days.

Two author figures appear and are kept apart: the period's, from the counts, and the authors of the posts read, counted from their handles. A post with no handle is in neither: give the number of such posts beside the authors.

With no posts to read in the free form, the posts reference gives the wording for a profile with no count and for one with a count of zero. This skill adds to the first "That is not the same as nobody praising it", and to the second "LMTY began collecting posts for it on 2024-11-02; the record does not say it continues". A profile cannot show that its phrases were removed. Signed in, the phrase list can: then write "its phrases were removed on 2025-02-13" in place of the second half.

## When the user points at posts

"Is this one worth quoting?" and "what about these three?" are not a search for praise. The user has chosen the posts, and the answer is about those.

| The user gives | Read |
| --- | --- |
| A day, or a day and an author | That day's posts, narrowed to the platform when it is known |
| A link or an author, with no day | The newest pages, up to 4. Not found: ask for the day or the text |
| The text itself, pasted | Nothing. Judge the text as pasted, and say that is what was judged |

Narrow the read to the product's own phrases when it has some. When it has none, read without that narrowing: the pages are then about other products, which is why a search for praise does not read them, and it is where the posts the user saw are.

Each post gets the same tests as a candidate and the same form: the excerpt as returned, the author group, and the three things not verified. Then one line, "worth a look" or "not a candidate", with the reason in the words of the test it failed: "praise of Northwind, not of Acme", "the company's own account", "the praise, if any, is after the cut".

No count stands beside the answer. The posts read to find the three are not a sample of anything, so there is no "N of M", no "Set aside" table and no further candidate. Asked to confirm that an author is a customer, say what the post shows, as for any candidate.

## What the free form leans toward

The most engaged posts of a busy product are not its typical posts. They lean toward large accounts, the company's own announcements, jokes and arguments. Praise from an ordinary user with eleven likes is collected and not shown.

So the free form on a large product often returns few candidates from 100 posts, and most of the 100 set aside. That is the correct result. Say it in one clause: "the posts shown are the most engaged, which favors announcements and large accounts over ordinary users".

On a small product the posts shown may be all of them. Then the count collected and the count read are the same number, and the answer says "all 37".

## Is it praise of the product?

Read the excerpt. A tag or a sentiment mark finds a post; it does not decide.

| The excerpt | Keep? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Acme's audit log saved us during the review last week." | Yes | Favorable, about the product, first-hand |
| "Acme is great." | Yes, at the lowest level | Favorable, no reason |
| "Northwind is so much better than Acme now." | No | Praise of a competitor that names the product |
| "Huge thanks to the Globex team for getting our Acme rollout done in a week." | No | Praise that mainly praises someone else |
| "Acme is fine but the pricing is killing us." | No | A favorable word inside a complaint |
| "Has anyone tried Acme? Looks promising." | No | A question, and no experience stated |
| "Thrilled to announce I'm joining Acme!" | No | The author's own news, and they say they work there |
| "5 reasons Acme will transform your workflow. Link in bio." | No | Promotion |
| "We moved from Initech to Acme and" | No, described | The praise, if any, comes after the cut: "may continue" |
| A post in another language that reads as favorable | Yes | Quoted as posted. A gist may follow, marked as your reading |

When in doubt, it is not a candidate. A list of four solid leads is worth more than ten that a person has to argue with.

## How specific is the praise?

| Level | The post | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Names a feature and says what it did | "The scheduled exports in Acme cut our month-end close from three days to one." |
| 2 | Names a feature, or says what it did | "Acme's search is the first one that finds what I mean." |
| 3 | Says the product is good, with a reason | "Switched to Acme because setup took ten minutes." |
| 4 | Approval with no reason | "Love Acme." |

Order by level, then newest first. Followers and engagement never move a post up. A level 4 post from an account with 200,000 followers sits below a level 1 post from an account with 40.

A figure inside praise ("cut our time in half") is the author's claim. Quote it as theirs. Whether it holds is another skill's question.

## The cut

An excerpt is the opening of a post, and praise is often in the second half. The posts reference says under "How a cut shows" how to tell a cut excerpt in each source, and that a link ending in an ellipsis is a shortened address. For a candidate:

- the excerpt was cut, or stops mid-sentence: write "may continue" after the quote. An ellipsis that came with the excerpt is part of what was returned: quote it
- the excerpt was not cut, as far as it shows: add nothing. "may continue" does not go on every quote
- the praise is inside the excerpt: quote the excerpt as returned, whole, and stop where it stops
- the excerpt only sets up the praise: it is not a candidate. Describe it in the "Set aside" count as "may continue past the opening", and give the link so a person can read the rest
- never finish the sentence, never supply the word that obviously comes next, and never join two posts by one author into one quote

## Sorting authors for this job

The posts reference says under "Authors" where the record names a company's own accounts and which roles count. Read the accounts listed on the product's overview and on the company's once, and compare every author's handle with them, on the same platform, whatever its capitals.

| What the record or the post shows | Group | Candidate? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The handle is listed there under any role but a community: the company's, the product's, a founder's, an executive's, an employee's or a personal one | The company | No |
| "I work at Acme", or a company role, in the post | The company | No |
| A reseller, an agency, an integration partner or a consultant who implements the product | A partner or a vendor | No |
| A person describing their own work with the product, with nothing tying them to the company | An independent poster | Yes |
| Nothing that shows who they are | Unknown affiliation | Yes, with the group shown |
| A handle that carries the product's name and is not listed | Unknown affiliation. Note "the handle carries the product's name; the record does not list it" | Yes, with the note |
| A display name such as "Dana (Globex)" | Whatever the rest shows. The display name alone settles nothing | Yes, with the display name as given |
| No handle at all | Unknown affiliation: "no handle given; could not be checked against the official accounts" | Yes, with that said |

A post in a community the record lists is sorted by its author.

An employee whose account the record does not list, with nothing in the post that says so, is unknown affiliation. Say it once under the candidates: "the record lists some of the company's accounts; anyone else who works there cannot be told apart from other authors".

Unknown affiliation is expected to be the largest group. It is not "independent": an account that does not visibly promote the company is not thereby free of it.

A post with no handle is common on some platforms. It adds nothing to any count of authors: give "from 81 authors; 6 posts had no handle".

Do not look an author up: not their profile page, not a search, not another network. The group comes from what LMTY returned.

## Three things not verified

Every candidate carries the same three lines, because they are true of every candidate:

- **Employer:** not verified. The record holds no employer for any author. A company in a display name is part of the display name
- **Customer:** not verified. Write "says they use it" only when the post says so. Never "customer", "user" or "buyer" on your own word
- **Permission to reuse:** not asked. A public post is not a release

When the user asks to confirm one of these, say what the post shows and stop there. The person can answer. So can the user's own records, which the user reads.

## Set aside

One line per reason, with a count and one example, so the reader can see the list was filtered and how.

| Line | Signed in | Free |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Matched no phrase of the product's own | Only where the server did not narrow the read: by the post's matched keywords | Does not apply: every post shown is the profile's |
| The company's own posts | By the first-party tag, by the official accounts, then by reading | By the official accounts, then by reading |
| Partners and vendors | By reading | By reading |
| Promotional posts | By the promotional and directory tags, then by reading | By reading |
| Machine-written posts | By LMTY's tag for them | By reading, only when plain; otherwise not judged |
| Praise that mainly praises someone else | By reading | By reading |
| Marked positive, not praise of the product when read | By reading | Does not apply: nothing is marked |
| Not praise | Does not apply: only positive posts were read | By reading. Usually the largest line |
| May continue past the opening | By reading | By reading |

The counts in this table and the number of candidates add up to the posts read. If they do not, a post was counted twice.

With no candidates, name the line that removed the most: "no candidates in the 100 posts read; 71 were not praise and 18 were the company's own".

## Engagement and followers

Report what the post carries: likes, comments, followers when given. They say how far a post reached. They are not agreement, and they do not rank a candidate.

## Repeat runs

A repeat run lists only posts dated after the last run. Match by the post's link: a post already listed is not listed again, even when it is still among the most engaged. With no post dated after the last run: "no new candidates since [date]", in one line.

Signed in, begin the period at the date of the last run. The free form always covers the last 30 days, so a run more than 30 days after the last one has a stretch nobody read: say so.

## How to ask

One line in the answer, and no more: a person at the company writes to the author, quotes the exact words, says where they would appear, and waits for a yes in writing.

When the user asks for the message itself, write a draft of two or three sentences that thanks the author, quotes them exactly, names the use, and asks. Mark it as a draft for a person to review and send. Send nothing.

## What not to write

| Do not write | Because | Write |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Customers love the new dashboard." | Posts are not customers | "3 posts praise the dashboard. None says the author is a customer." |
| "Dana from Globex says..." | A display name is not an employer | "An account with the display name 'Dana (Globex)' says..." |
| "...cut our close from three days to one, which saved the team hours." | The second half was added | The excerpt as returned, and "may continue" |
| "A top influencer endorsed you." | Reach is not weight, and "endorsed" is a claim | "One post is from an account with 80,000 followers." |
| "Here are ten ready-to-use testimonials." | Nothing here is cleared for use | "Ten leads. None is verified or cleared for reuse." |
| "Nobody is praising you." | An empty read is a count | "No candidates in the 100 posts read." |
| "No praise of Acme this month." (from an account with no phrase for Acme) | Nothing was collected for it | "LMTY monitors no phrase for Acme itself." |
| "An Acme employee posted this." (from a handle with "acme" in it) | The record does not list the handle | "Unknown affiliation; the handle carries the product's name." |

## Length

The candidates and the "Set aside" table carry the read. Outside them, under 300 words. Ten candidates at most. With none, the short form: the coverage line, the one finding, the table.
