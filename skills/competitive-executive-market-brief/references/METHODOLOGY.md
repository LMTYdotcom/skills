# Executive Market Brief Methodology

## Executive selection rule

The brief is not "what happened." It is "what happened that could alter an assumption, decision, or risk worth executive attention."

## Consequence matrix

Consider each item on:
- strategic relevance
- customer/deal evidence
- magnitude
- urgency
- confidence
- reversibility

High-consequence, low-confidence issues often belong as research or decision questions rather than recommendations.

## Executive audience variants

### CEO / board

Emphasize:
- category and business-model movement
- strategic risk
- material competitor changes
- market openings
- assumptions behind company strategy

### CMO / PMM leader

Emphasize:
- positioning
- messaging
- GTM
- pricing narrative
- market signal-to-noise

### CPO / product leadership

Emphasize:
- customer expectations
- product/category shifts
- roadmap assumptions
- launches

### CRO / sales leadership

Emphasize:
- competitor prevalence in deals
- pricing/packaging
- objections
- enablement gaps
- win/loss signals

## Anti-newsletter rule

If an item has no plausible decision consequence for the audience, omit it or move it to an appendix.
