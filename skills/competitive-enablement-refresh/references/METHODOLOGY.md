# Sales Enablement Refresh Methodology

## Core principle

Enablement decays one claim at a time, and reps do not know which claim has gone. The refresh finds the ones that have, fixes exactly those, and tells reps what changed and why.

The failure modes are rewriting everything (nobody reads the new version, and the good lines got worse) and updating nothing (a rep quotes last year's price).

## Claims, not assets

An asset is a container. The unit of work is the claim: a sentence a rep might say to a buyer because the asset told them to.

Pull the claims out before classifying. A twelve-slide deck may have six claims that matter; a one-page talk track may have twenty. Duplicated claims across assets are one claim with several homes - classify it once and fix it everywhere it lives.

## The four classes

**Current.** Supported by approved proof or current evidence; wording right; date acceptable. Leave it alone and say so.

**Stale.** Was true, is not now, because something moved: a price, a tier, a certification, an integration, a customer reference that left. Always has a change behind it, with a date. The fix is the new fact, its proof, and a history note.

**Unsupported.** Never had approved proof or evidence behind it. The driving change did not break it; it revealed that nothing was holding it up. "Over 10,000 teams have switched" with no source; "we're SOC 2" when the approved claims doc says certification status comes from security. The fix is the supportable version, or removal.

**Incomplete.** Correct, and silent on something the field is now asked. The gap is defined by the field questions, not by what the PMM thinks should be there. The fix is an addition scoped to the question.

Unsupported is the class teams resist, because the claim is often true. That is not the point: a rep making a true claim they cannot back is one buyer question away from a credibility loss.

## Field consequence

Priority is the cost of a rep saying the old line today, not the size of the edit:

1. a number a buyer can check - price, tier, seat limit
2. a capability claim the buyer will test in a demo or POC
3. a security or compliance assertion the buyer's security team will verify
4. a customer reference that has churned or never approved the mention
5. a competitor weakness that is no longer true
6. framing and tone

One wrong price in a talk track outranks a slide with a dated screenshot.

## The smallest useful update

The replacement text should be the minimum that makes the claim true and supportable:

- the new line
- the proof, named
- the date

Not a rewrite of the surrounding section. Not new claims the refresh was not asked for. Not a change of voice. If the asset needs a rewrite, say so as a separate recommendation and route it.

## Reversals

When the new line contradicts the old one, reps who learned the old one will hold both until one wins. Give them the history:

> Until September 2026 this card said Monday.com's enterprise pricing was sales-gated. On 2026-09-09 they published it and removed the Enterprise tier. If a buyer quotes the old line back to you, that is why.

A reversal without history creates a rep who says the old thing with more confidence.

## Requests for unsupportable lines

Sales will sometimes ask for a line the evidence cannot carry: a comparison the approved claims do not support, a certification not yet listed, a "faster than X" nobody measured. The answer is not a hedged version of the same claim. It is:

- what the evidence does support, written as the line
- what would be needed to support the requested line
- who owns getting it

## Content or distribution

Before adding a paragraph, ask whether the paragraph already exists. Signals that the problem is reach, not content:

- the asset answers the question and the question keeps coming
- usage data shows the asset unopened
- the question comes from reps who joined after the last training
- the asset lives somewhere reps do not look

A distribution problem gets a distribution fix: a post in the channel reps read, a five-minute session, a pin. Adding the same answer in a second document does not help.

If there is no usage data, say the question is open rather than assuming content.

## What not to optimize for

Do not optimize for:

- the number of sections changed
- a fresh-looking deck
- answering every field request with a new claim
- completeness of the update

Optimize for:

- every stale number fixed, with proof and date
- unsupported claims named as such, however true
- the highest-consequence line first
- history where a claim reversed
- knowing whether reps will see it
