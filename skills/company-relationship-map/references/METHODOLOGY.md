# Relationship Map Methodology

## A tie has two ends and a speaker

"Northwind partners with Initech" could rest on Northwind's partner page, Initech's, a joint announcement, a press article, or one post. Each supports a different sentence.

| Stated by | Write | Supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Both parties | "partners, per both companies" | A mutual, announced relationship |
| One party only | "Northwind lists Initech as a partner" | That Northwind says so |
| A directory or marketplace | "listed in Initech's integration directory" | That a connector exists |
| The press | "reported to have partnered" | The report |
| One post | "unverified: one post says" | Nothing beyond the post |

The grade says a party spoke; it does not say which. For partners, integrations and rivals the tie's direction does not say either, and only its sources do. Read them before writing the sentence. The sources, and whether a start date is stated, are in the profile's connections section, not on the ties read around the center.

## Three kinds with no direction

Ties of kind `competes_with`, `integrates_with` and `partners_with` are held with their two parties in an arbitrary order. Which end a company sits at means nothing for these three. A tie that points out from a company is not thereby that company's own naming, listing or announcement.

| To say | Read |
| --- | --- |
| Who named the rival | The tie's sources: a source on Northwind's site is Northwind naming Initech |
| Whose directory lists the integration | The tie's sources: the site the listing is on |
| Who announced the partnership | The tie's sources: one party's site, the other's, or both |
| Any of these, with no source read | Nothing: write the tie as existing between the two, "stated by: not read" |

Compare each source's site with each party's site. A source on neither is a third party's: the press, or an outside list.

For every other kind (customer, investor, founder, leader, acquirer, subsidiary) the direction carries the meaning: who invested in whom, who acquired whom.

## The kinds, and what each one is evidence of

**Founded, leads, board, advises.** Roles of people. Current unless ended or marked former. A founder who left is still a founder.

**Invested in.** An investor in a round. Pair the investor with a round only where a source names that investor in that round; a tie's date falling near a round's date does not place it there. Otherwise list investors and rounds separately. It does not carry the stake. An investor stays on the map after later rounds; "still holds" is not known.

**Acquired, subsidiary of.** Ownership. Read the date and whether a source calls it completed or announced. An announced deal is not a closed one.

**Partners with.** The loosest word in business. Without both parties and a description, it supports only "lists as a partner".

**Integrates with.** A product connection. Usually one-sided and usually just a listing. Hundreds of them are normal and say little individually; count them and name the few that matter to the question.

**Competes with.** Always somebody's framing: the company's own comparison page, a rival's, or a third party's list. Three useful distinctions:

- who the company names (who it wants to be compared with)
- who names the company (who sees it as a rival)
- named by both

A competitor named by nobody but an outside list is the list's view.

The direction of the tie does not show which of the three it is. Go by the tie's sources, as "Three kinds with no direction" says. With none read, the rival is listed with "named by: not read".

## Choosing what to show

A well-covered company has hundreds of ties. Show by the question:

| Question | Show | Count the rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who backs it? | Investors with their dates, and a round only where a source names the investor in it; rounds; owners; board | - |
| Who does it work with? | Partners stated by both sides; the integrations most relevant to the reader | Integrations by number |
| Who does it compete with? | The three groups above | - |
| Ecosystem overview | Five or six per group, best evidenced first | Everything else, as totals per kind |
| How are A and B connected? | Every path of one or two steps | - |

On every answer state read N of M for each kind read, so the reader knows how much is behind the handful shown. Give no count for a kind that was not requested or read, such as customers or employment.

A tie's grade decides its label, never whether it is shown. Among the kinds read, every tie that bears on the question appears, named or counted; an unverified tie is shown, labeled unverified. Writing that no tie of a kind was observed while leaving one out is a false statement.

A kind with more than 10 ties is counted, not listed, and every grade group is accounted for:

- the count for each grade
- a grade group of three or fewer named in full, so a single reported rival among 20 official ones stays in sight
- from a larger group, the few that matter most: the ties with the most sources, then the higher grade. Recency only breaks a tie, because on a young profile it is the order LMTY read the pages in
- what is not shown as a number only, never as a list of names
- ended ties counted separately from current ones

Sections with nothing in them are left out, and the one "Not on the map" line names each of the four groups (people, money and ownership, working ties, rivals) that has no tie: "not observed in LMTY's sources" when the kind was read and empty, "not read" when it was not requested. Never "not assessed", "none" or "has no".

Leaving out empty sections never removes a tie. Every tie that was read is named with both parties and the way it reads, including a tie of a kind the question did not ask about when it is all the profile holds. A profile with three ties or fewer gets its freshness line, those ties with their grades and who states each, and the one "Not on the map" line: no other section, no explanation of tie kinds, and no more than about 150 words. The web section, when something was taken from outside LMTY, comes after and is not counted.

## Dates

- stated start: "since 2021"
- no stated start: "seen since 2024-10-13"
- stated end: "until 2023, per [source]"
- no longer listed: "no longer listed as of [date]"
- nothing recent: "as of [last date a source said so]"

Most first-seen dates sit on the day a profile was first read. That is not when the ties formed.

## Paths between two

Read two steps out from one of them and look for the other, or for a shared neighbor.

Report each path as its links:

> Northwind - Meridian Ventures - Initech: Meridian Ventures led Northwind's Series A (reported, 2023-05) and appears in Initech's seed round (official, Initech's site, 2021).

Rank paths by their weakest link. A path through one unverified tie is an unverified path, and it is still given: every tie found on a path between the two is reported, whatever its grade.

State read N of M for each kind read, for each of the two centers. "Other paths may exist" tells the reader nothing without it.

What a path is not:

- a shared investor is not coordination
- a shared former employer is not a relationship between the companies
- a mutual integration partner is not a partnership

If there is no path within two steps, say "no connection observed within two steps", not "unconnected".

## People on the map

Founders, leaders, board members and advisers appear by role and dates. That is all the map holds on a person, and all it should.

## Length

One table per group. A paragraph of prose at most, at the top, naming the three or four ties a reader most needs. Maps invite completeness; resist it.
