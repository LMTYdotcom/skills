# Internal context

The skills refer to internal sources by capability, never by vendor. A
CRM is "opportunity data"; a call recorder is "call intelligence"; a
wiki, drive or enablement library is "company knowledge" or "the
enablement repository". If such a source is connected, its tools are in
your tool list; look before asking.

Market truth and internal truth answer different questions and must
stay distinguishable in everything you write. LMTY and public evidence
say what is true of the market. Internal sources say what this company
recorded, said, promised, won and lost. Neither corrects the other by
itself; when they disagree, that disagreement is a finding.

## What each source is evidence of

**Call intelligence** is evidence of what was said. A buyer's words are
the best available record of the buyer's concern and vocabulary. They
are not proof that a product claim inside them is true - a buyer who
says "Northwind has more integrations" has told you what they believe and
why it matters to them, not what Northwind has.

**Opportunity data** is evidence of what the team captured. Stage,
value, segment and dates are usually reliable. Competitor fields, loss
reasons and risk reasons are rep-entered: they record what one person
wrote at one moment, which is often the justification rather than the
cause. A field that has not been updated in weeks describes the deal as
it was then. Say when a field was last updated whenever it matters.

**Win/loss research** is evidence of what the buyer said after the
decision, through an interviewer. Prefer the buyer's quoted words; treat
the interviewer's paraphrase as the interviewer's. It is the strongest
source for *why the buyer decided*, and the sample is almost always
small and non-representative - say how small.

**Company knowledge** - approved claims, pricing, security documentation,
customer references, policies - is evidence of what the company will
stand behind. It outranks improvised rep language and anything in an
older enablement asset. It can be stale; carry its date.

**Existing enablement** - battlecards, decks, talk tracks - is an
artifact to verify, not a source. Its claims were true, or thought
true, when written. Check them against approved proof and current
market state before repeating any of them, and name the ones that no
longer hold.

**Product analytics, usage and telemetry** are evidence of what users
did, within whatever the instrumentation covers. They do not say why,
and they do not see users who left or never arrived.

**Team communication** - channels, threads, meeting notes - is evidence
that someone said something, with the least editorial control of any
source. Useful for what people are worried about and when; weak for what
is true.

## Rules that apply to every skill

- **One account is one account.** One call, one deal, one interview is a
  single observation. Label evidence as a single example, a repeated
  pattern across a stated N, or a quantified pattern across a defined
  cohort. Never call something "common", "typical" or "usually" from
  one instance.
- **A source's interpretation is that source's.** A rep's loss reason,
  an interviewer's paraphrase, LMTY's implication sentence, a
  battlecard's claim: quote it and say whose it is. Do not restate it
  as an observed fact or as your own finding.
- **Conflict is a finding.** When the CRM says price and the call says
  security, show both, say which source is stronger for which question,
  and do not average them into "price and security".
- **Do not invent deal history.** No customer stories, win patterns,
  "what usually works", or prior examples unless a connected source or
  the user supplied them.
- **Access is not permission to expose.** An internal source may contain
  account names, contract values, personal details, or another
  customer's private situation. Use the minimum the job needs, keep the
  source system's access boundary, and never turn one customer's private
  situation into a general claim in an artifact that will travel.
- **Retrieve only what the job needs.** A live question does not
  warrant a full-corpus dump. Good order: the named record or call,
  then the relevant account or opportunity, then the current market
  fact, then matching approved proof, then prior examples only if
  needed.
- **Internal claims still need approved proof when they are material.**
  A rep's note that "we have SOC 2" is a note. The approved security
  documentation is proof.

## When internal sources are absent

Every skill still works. Say once, in the mode or freshness line, that
no internal context was available, and then do not:

- claim an objection or pattern is common
- say what "usually" wins or loses
- invent prior deals, quotes, or customer examples
- present a market-only answer as if it reflected this company's field
  reality
